Don't just memorize the universe. Investigate it.

AstroReach is an independent astronomy initiative for middle school students. We teach the questions astronomers ask, and how to answer them with real data.

How learning works here

Ask

Every lesson starts with a real question, like how we can tell a planet is hiding in a star's light.

Explore

Students change a graph, test an idea, and watch what happens before any rule is explained.

Explain

They put the pattern into words, then connect it to what astronomers actually do.

Science is a way of thinking, not a list of facts

Students often meet astronomy as vocabulary: planets, orbits, galaxies. AstroReach aims to move past that, so students learn to make a claim, check it against evidence, and change their mind when the data says so.

Every activity uses real concepts and data sources, and we credit where images and data come from.

Finding a planet in a star's light

Astronomers often find planets they can't see. When a planet passes in front of its star, the star gets a little dimmer. Change the planet's size and watch the dip.

Investigate

1. Make the planet twice as wide. Does the dip get twice as deep? Write down your prediction first.
2. Find the smallest planet whose dip you could still notice on a noisy graph.
3. Why might it be easier to find big planets than small ones?

Note: this graph is a simplified model for learning, not real telescope data. Real data lessons are planned.
